In 1983, a warehouse in Seattle started charging people for the right to shop there. Twenty-five dollars a year, just to walk through the door. Most retailers thought it was a gimmick.

Today, Costco collects around five billion dollars a year in membership fees. And here is the strange part: that money is almost pure profit.

The products on the shelves? Costco barely makes anything on them. Its markup is capped at around fifteen percent, far below what a typical supermarket charges.

So what is Costco really selling? Not groceries. Not televisions. It is selling a promise: that you will never overpay. And as long as members believe that promise, they keep renewing. About nine out of ten of them, every single year.

This is The Cost Curve. Let's follow the money.
