## 0. Hook

> Logo Ryanair (usage informatif) sur un 737 au décollage. Une étiquette de prix géante : « €14.99 ».

In April 2026, Ryanair sold flights across Europe from fourteen euros and ninety-nine cents. [R1]

> Deux barres : billet moyen 55,70 € contre coût par passager ≈ 61 €.

Here's the strange part. By our calculation from Ryanair's own accounts, flying one passenger costs it about sixty-one euros. [R31] And the average ticket, not the sale price, the average, brought in less than fifty-six euros this summer. [R29]

> Bénéfice semestriel : 2,15 Md€.

So the ticket doesn't even cover the cost of the flight. And yet, in those same six months, Ryanair made two point one five billion euros of profit. [R29]

> Le parcours du passager se dessine : 7 arrêts, de l'écran de recherche à l'atterrissage.

The ticket is the bait. The money is somewhere else. So let's buy one of those cheap seats, follow it from the search page to the runway, and keep the receipt. At every stop, we'll write down what Ryanair earns, and what it spends.

> Ident The Cost Curve, 3 s.

## 1. Stop 1: the price on the screen

> Écran de réservation stylisé (graphique de la chaîne, pas une capture du site) : « €14.99 ».

Our trip starts on a search page, with a price that looks too low to be real.

> Chiffres : 208 M de passagers, 94 % des sièges remplis.

Ryanair carries more passengers than any other airline in Europe. In its last full financial year, to March 2026, it flew two hundred and eight million people, and filled ninety-four percent of its seats. [R2] [R9]

That full year includes a winter, a season Ryanair itself describes as loss-making. [R15] So for our receipt, we'll use the full year, and divide everything by the number of passengers.

> Le reçu commence : billet ≈ 51 €, coût ≈ 63 €, écart −12 €.

The average ticket brought in about fifty-one euros. [R3] Flying each passenger cost about sixty-three. [R3] On the ticket alone, Ryanair was about twelve euros short, for every single passenger.

And yet the airline made a profit of more than two billion euros that year. [R2] About eleven euros per passenger, after tax. [R3]

So somewhere between the search page and the runway, every passenger hands over more than twenty euros on top of the ticket. The first clue is on the very next screen.

## 2. Stop 2: checkout

> Liste animée : bagage, siège, priorité, assurance, voiture, hôtel.

You've picked your flight. Before you can pay, Ryanair offers you a few things. A bigger bag. A seat of your choice. Priority boarding. A rental car, a hotel.

> Le reçu : + options ≈ 24 €. Barres empilées billets / options.

Ryanair calls these "ancillary revenues". In its last full year, they reached almost five billion euros, about twenty-four euros per passenger. [R2] That's around a third of everything the airline earns. [R3] This summer, it was close to twenty-five euros per passenger. [R29]

> Barres 2019 contre 2026 : passagers +50 %, options × 2.

And the extras have grown faster than the airline. In 2019, they brought in a little over seventeen euros per passenger. [R5] Since then, the number of passengers has grown by half. Revenue from extras has more than doubled. [R5] [R9]

There's a reason the price is split this way. The ticket price is what people compare on search sites. The extras come later, once you've already chosen. So Ryanair can fight hard on the headline number, and earn its margin after the click.

> Logo Booking.com ; mot « pirates » entre guillemets.

That only works if you book on Ryanair's own website. Which is why Ryanair has spent years fighting the online travel agencies that resell its seats without a deal, sites it has called "pirates". [R24] [R25]

> Carte : l'Italie s'allume. 256 M€, payée en septembre 2026, en appel.

In December 2025, Italy's competition authority fined Ryanair two hundred and fifty-six million euros for abusing its dominant position against travel agents, including by blocking their bookings and asking their customers for facial verification. [R24] Ryanair calls it "legally flawed", paid it in September 2026, and will get the money back if it wins its appeal. [R24] [R36]

## 3. Stop 3: check-in

> Photo : borne d'enregistrement, téléphone avec carte d'embarquement.

Paid. Now, check-in. And here, some charges seem designed less to be paid than to change what you do.

> Étiquette de prix : 20 € → 55 €.

Since November 2025, Ryanair only accepts digital boarding passes. Forget to check in online, ask for a pass at the airport, and it costs fifty-five euros per passenger, per flight, in most of Europe. It used to be twenty. [R22] Almost nobody should pay it. The point is that everybody checks in on the app: no counter to staff, and one more chance to sell you a seat.

> Photo : gabarit à bagages.

Then there's the bag. The cheapest fare includes one small bag under the seat. Anything bigger costs extra, and regulators are pushing back.

> Carte : l'Espagne s'allume. 107 M€, suspendue par un tribunal.

In 2024, Spain's consumer ministry fined Ryanair one hundred and seven million euros over practices it called abusive, including charging for cabin bags and for printing boarding passes. [R23] Michael O'Leary called the fine "illegal and baseless", and in June 2025, a Madrid court suspended it while the case went on. [R23]

> Photo : Parlement européen. Deux règles : bagage à main inclus, enfants assis à côté d'un parent.

In July 2026, the European Parliament approved new passenger-rights rules. Prices shown online will have to include a free item of hand luggage, and airlines won't be allowed to charge families to seat children under fourteen next to a parent. The rules are expected to apply from 2027. [R26]

## 4. Stop 4: an airport that wanted you

> Photo : aéroport régional, un 737 Ryanair sur le tarmac.

Now, the airport. And it may not be the one you'd expect.

> Barres : aéroports et assistance par passager, Ryanair 8 €, Wizz 15 €, easyJet 27 €, IAG 50 €.

By Ryanair's own comparison, it pays about eight euros per passenger for airports and ground handling. easyJet pays twenty-seven. [R7] Part of the reason is where it flies: often to smaller, regional airports, which compete to get its planes.

For a regional airport, a Ryanair base means tourists and jobs. So some have offered discounts, and sometimes more: contracts in which the airport, or local authorities, pay Ryanair's marketing subsidiary to promote their region on Ryanair's website.

> Carte de France : Angoulême, Pau, Nîmes, Montpellier. Angoulême : 400 000 € (2008), 300 000 € (2009), 225 000 € (2010) = 925 000 €.

The European Commission has looked at several of these deals. At Angoulême, in western France, the airport paid that subsidiary more than nine hundred thousand euros over three years, under a contract tied to a new route to London. [R20] In 2014, the Commission ruled that Ryanair had received illegal state aid there, and at Pau and Nîmes. [R19] In 2019, it ordered France to recover about eight and a half million euros paid to Ryanair at Montpellier, calling it "an unfair and selective advantage over its competitors". [R21]

> Charleroi : décision annulée (2008). Bordeaux : avion immobilisé, 525 000 €.

Ryanair has contested these decisions, and it hasn't lost them all. In 2008, an EU court annulled the Commission's ruling on Charleroi, in Belgium. [R18] But in November 2018, French authorities grounded a Ryanair plane at Bordeaux to recover the Angoulême money. Ryanair paid five hundred and twenty-five thousand euros to get it back. [R20]

> Carte : flèches vers la Suède, l'Italie, l'Albanie, le Maroc ; retrait d'Allemagne, d'Autriche, d'Espagne régionale. Charleroi 19 → 12, Vienne 20 → 14.

Today, the game is played between countries. Ryanair says it's moving planes to places that cut aviation taxes, like Sweden, Italy, Albania and Morocco, and away from Germany, Austria and regional Spain. [R17] This winter, its base at Charleroi drops from nineteen planes to twelve, and Vienna from twenty to fourteen. [R34] The planes go where the fees are lowest.

## 5. Stop 5: the plane

> Photo : flotte de 737 Ryanair alignés. Chiffre : ≈ 650 avions, 621 Boeing 737.

Up the stairs and onto the plane. Almost certainly a Boeing 737. Of Ryanair's roughly six hundred and fifty aircraft, all but twenty-six are 737s. [R10] [R36] One type means one kind of training and one set of spare parts.

> Chronologie : 2002, 2021, 2023, 2026, 2027.

How Ryanair buys them matters even more. Rule one: buy when nobody else is buying. In January 2002, four months after the September eleventh attacks, Boeing was having what its own sales chief called a difficult year. Ryanair ordered one hundred 737s, with options for fifty more, worth nine point one billion dollars at catalogue prices. [R12] Boeing called its own offer "exceptionally competitive". [R12]

> Photo : Gamechanger. 189 → 197 sièges, −16 % de carburant par siège. Puis MAX 10.

Rule two: squeeze in more seats. The version Ryanair calls the "Gamechanger" has one hundred and ninety-seven seats instead of one hundred and eighty-nine, and burns sixteen percent less fuel per seat. [R10] The next one, the bigger 737 MAX 10, was finally certified by America's aviation regulator in October 2026, after years of delays. Ryanair expects European approval by the end of the year, and its first sixteen in spring 2027. [R35] It has ordered up to three hundred, worth forty billion dollars at list prices, and says each one has twenty percent more seats and is twenty percent more fuel-efficient. [R11] [R35]

> Chiffre : 620 Boeing sans dette. Barres : possession et maintenance par passager, Ryanair 9 €, easyJet 15 €, Wizz 20 €.

Rule three: pay cash. Six hundred and twenty of its Boeings carry no debt or mortgage, and since repaying its last bond in May 2026, Ryanair has described itself as debt-free. [R36] [R13] No lease payments, almost no interest. By Ryanair's count, owning and maintaining its planes costs nine euros per passenger. At Wizz Air, twenty. [R7]

## 6. Stop 6: in the air

> Photo : avitaillement. Donut : carburant 44 % des coûts du semestre.

Doors closed, engines on. And here, our receipt hits the one big cost Ryanair can't squeeze: fuel.

Fuel was about forty-one percent of Ryanair's costs in its last full year, and forty-four percent this summer. [R6] [R30] To protect itself, Ryanair buys most of its fuel in advance. Until March 2027, eighty percent is locked in at about sixty-seven dollars a barrel. [R32]

> Courbe : 80 % à 67 $, 20 % non couverts qui doublent.

The other twenty percent is not. And in 2026, the price of that unhedged fuel roughly doubled. [R32] [R14]

> Tuiles H1 FY27 : passagers +6 %, billet −4 %, coûts +10 %, bénéfice −15 % à 2,15 Md€.

On November second, Ryanair published the result. Over the summer half, it flew six percent more passengers, but average fares fell four percent, costs rose ten, and profit fell fifteen percent, to two point one five billion euros. [R29]

> Barres : couverture FY27 80 % à 67 $, FY28 25 % à 95 $. Puis +2 Md€, ≈ +10 € par passager.

And the hedge runs out. For next year, only a quarter of its fuel is locked in, at around ninety-five dollars a barrel. [R32] Ryanair estimates its fuel bill could rise by about two billion euros. To pass that on, it says, fares would need to rise by about ten euros per passenger. [R32] Michael O'Leary's verdict: "There is no way it is going back down to the sixty-seven dollars a barrel." [R32]

> Photo : tour de contrôle. Tuiles : redevances de navigation +9 %, taxes environnementales ≈ 1,4 Md€.

Air traffic control charges rose nine percent too. [R30] And Ryanair expects EU environmental taxes to cost it about one point four billion euros this year. [R17]

## 7. Stop 7: the crew

> Photo : équipage Ryanair. Barres : personnel par passager, Ryanair 9 €, easyJet 15 €, IAG 54 €.

Look around the cabin: a few flight attendants, two pilots, and another big difference in Ryanair's costs. By Ryanair's own comparison, its staff cost nine euros per passenger. easyJet: fifteen. IAG: fifty-four. [R7] Full planes and quick turnarounds mean more passengers per crew.

> Chronologie courte : 2017, 20 000 vols annulés, syndicats reconnus ; arrêt de la CJUE.

It also comes with pressure. For decades, Ryanair refused to recognize unions. That changed in December 2017, after a pilot rostering mistake forced it to cancel about twenty thousand flights. Facing its first pilot strikes, it agreed to recognize unions for the first time in its history. [R27]

> Grand graphique : coût hors carburant par passager, Ryanair 36 € contre six concurrents.

Now put the whole cost machine together. Take fuel out, since every airline pays roughly the same market price for it, and here's what it costs to fly one passenger, by Ryanair's own comparison. Ryanair: thirty-six euros. Wizz Air: sixty-five. easyJet: ninety. Southwest: one hundred and forty-seven. IAG and Lufthansa: one hundred and seventy-eight. Air France-KLM: two hundred and fifty-three. [R33]

Those airlines don't all do the same thing. Long-haul flights and business class cost money. But on short flights in Europe, nobody comes close.

## 8. Landing: the receipt

> Le reçu complet, ligne par ligne : billet 51 €, options 24 €, coût 63 €, bénéfice 11 €.

Wheels down. Let's read the receipt for one passenger, over Ryanair's last full year. Ticket: about fifty-one euros. Extras: about twenty-four. The cost of flying you: about sixty-three. Left over: about eleven euros of profit. [R3]

> Le parcours complet, les 7 arrêts.

So, how does Ryanair make money on fifteen-euro flights? It doesn't, not on the ticket alone. The ticket gets you on board. The money comes from the extras you add after you've chosen, from costs that, by its own count, are the lowest in Europe, from planes bought in bad times and paid for in cash, and from airports and countries competing for its planes.

> Barres : marges approximatives, Ryanair, IAG, easyJet, Wizz Air.

That model has made Ryanair one of Europe's most profitable airlines, on a par with IAG, and far ahead of Wizz Air, which barely broke even last year. [R8] It also makes it a frequent target for regulators, and leaves it exposed to the one line on the receipt it can't control: the price of oil.

> La ligne « billet » s'efface du reçu, les autres restent.

The cheapest seat on the plane is rarely the reason the plane makes money.

> Écran de fin, 20 s : vidéo suivante + abonnement.
