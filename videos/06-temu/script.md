## 0. Hook

> Logo Temu (usage informatif) sur une photo de soute d'avion-cargo. Une étiquette de prix géante : « $2 ».

Two-dollar socks. A phone case for less than a coffee. Shipped from a factory in China to a front door in Ohio, for free.

> Chiffres : 13 M → 51 M d'utilisateurs mensuels aux États-Unis.

That was Temu's promise when it arrived in America in September 2022. [T1] Less than a year and a half later, fifty-one million Americans were using it every month, according to Sensor Tower. [T9]

> Une porte qui se ferme : « Duty-free parcels: closed ».

Then, in 2025, the United States shut the door that model was built on: the rule that let small parcels into the country without paying any customs duty. [T10] [T12]

> Question à l'écran : « Can Temu survive without duty-free shipping? »

So here's our question. How did Temu sell things that cheap in the first place? And can it survive without that door?

The usual answers are cheap factories, burning cash, and a flood of ads. Each one is partly true. None of them is the whole story.

> Ident The Cost Curve, 3 s.

## 1. Who is Temu?

> Photo de Shanghai la nuit. Logos Pinduoduo puis Temu, reliés à « PDD Holdings ».

Temu belongs to PDD Holdings, a company officially based in Dublin, whose roots, and most of its money, are in China. [T2]

Its other app is Pinduoduo, one of China's biggest shopping platforms. It started in 2015 with what it calls "team purchase": if enough friends buy a product together, everyone gets a lower price. [T28] PDD says Pinduoduo and Temu have "the same value propositions and operational model". [T2] Temu is Pinduoduo's playbook, exported.

> Carte : Boston (septembre 2022), Océanie (mars 2023), Europe (avril 2023).

Temu launched in the United States in September 2022, reached Australia six months later, and Europe a month after that. [T1]

> Barres : services de transaction PDD, 2022 → 2025, en milliards de yuans.

PDD doesn't publish separate accounts for Temu. But one line of its results includes Temu's marketplace fees: transaction services. In 2022, it brought in about twenty-eight billion yuan. By 2025, two hundred and fourteen billion. Almost eight times more in three years. [T3]

So whatever Temu did, it worked. Now, how?

## 2. False trail one: cheap factories

> Photo d'atelier textile. Mot à l'écran : « cheap labor? »

The first answer most people give: things are made in China, Chinese factories are cheap, so Temu is cheap.

> Logos côte à côte : Amazon, Walmart, Shein, AliExpress.

But Amazon sellers buy from Chinese factories too. So do Walmart's suppliers, and Shein, and AliExpress. The difference isn't where things are made. It's what happens after the factory door.

> Schéma : usine → importateur → grossiste → entrepôt → magasin → client. Les intermédiaires s'effacent.

A product sold in an American store usually passes through an importer, a distributor, a warehouse and a retailer, and each one adds its margin. Temu cut most of them out. The order went from the factory, more or less directly, to you.

> Citation : « sellers leave everything – price-setting, shipping and marketing included – to Temu ». Bloomberg, févr. 2025.

And under Temu's original model, known as "fully managed", sellers left everything, in Bloomberg's words, "price-setting, shipping and marketing included", to Temu. [T7] The factory supplied the goods. Temu decided what you'd pay.

> Citation d'une vendeuse : « We are working for Temu for free ». WIRED, 2023.

That gave Temu enormous pressure on prices. Sellers interviewed by WIRED in 2023 said they had no say over prices. One put it this way: "We are working for Temu for free". [T8]

So the factories were cheap, yes. But it was Temu that squeezed the margins out of everything around them.

## 3. False trail two: burning cash

> Mot à l'écran : « losing money on every order? »

The second answer: Temu was simply losing money on every order, to buy customers.

> Reçu d'une commande type 2023 : commande moyenne ≈ 25 $, colis Chine → États-Unis ≈ 14 $, perte estimée ≈ 30 $.

That's partly true, at least at the start. In May 2023, WIRED estimated that Temu was losing about thirty dollars per order in the United States. [T8] One brokerage figured that sending a single small parcel from Guangzhou to the US cost around fourteen dollars, on an average order of about twenty-five. [T8]

That's what a land grab looks like: pay for the first order, and hope the customer comes back. But it doesn't explain how Temu kept going for years.

> Barres : bénéfice net de PDD, 2022 → 2025 (31,5 / 60,0 / 112,4 / 99,4 Md RMB).

Look at its owner. PDD's profit didn't collapse while Temu expanded. It more than tripled, from thirty-one billion yuan in 2022 to one hundred and twelve billion in 2024. [T5]

> Chiffres : 4,0 Md$ de bénéfice au T2 2026 ; 67 Md$ de trésorerie.

Even from April to June 2026, PDD made four billion dollars of profit in a single quarter, and held sixty-seven billion dollars in cash and short-term investments. [T6]

So Temu wasn't a startup betting its last dollar. It was a very profitable Chinese company choosing to spend part of its profits abroad. And a lot of that money bought ads.

## 4. False trail three: the ads

> Photo du stade Allegiant, Super Bowl LVIII. Chiffres : 3 spots pendant le match, 2 après, 6,5-7 M$ le spot de 30 s.

At the Super Bowl in February 2024, Temu bought three ad slots during the game and two more after it, at roughly six and a half to seven million dollars for thirty seconds, plus more than fifteen million dollars in coupons and giveaways. [T9]

> Barres : dépenses marketing de PDD, 2022 → 2025 (54 / 82 / 111 / 125 Md RMB).

PDD's sales and marketing spending went from fifty-four billion yuan in 2022 to one hundred and twenty-five billion in 2025. [T4] That's more than seventeen billion dollars in a single year, mostly, the company says, on promotion and advertising. [T4]

> Chiffre : part de Temu dans les impressions Google Shopping : 20 %.

In early April 2025, Temu accounted for about a fifth of all ad impressions on Google Shopping in the United States, according to the ad agency Tinuiti. [T14]

But ads only bring people to the app. They don't make a phone case cost two dollars on your doorstep. For that, the parcel itself had to be cheap. And that's where the real answer lies.

## 5. The real answer: the parcel

> Photo de conteneurs aériens sur le tarmac. Texte : « Section 321 — de minimis: $800 ».

American customs law has a rule called "de minimis", from a Latin phrase about things too small to matter. It dates back to 1938. [T26] In 2016, Congress raised its limit from two hundred dollars to eight hundred. [T26] Any shipment under that line, imported by one person on one day, came in without duties or taxes, and with only minimal data for customs. [T26]

> Schéma : un conteneur de 10 000 articles (droits, déclaration complète) contre 10 000 petits colis (aucun droit).

Think about what that means. A store that imports ten thousand phone cases in a container pays duty on all of them, and files a full declaration. But ten thousand phone cases sent as ten thousand separate parcels, each to a different customer? No duty at all.

> Courbe : colis de minimis, 139 M (2015) → 1,36 Md (2024).

That was the heart of Temu's model. Every order left China as its own little parcel, mostly by air, straight to the buyer. In 2015, US customs handled one hundred and thirty-nine million de minimis shipments. In the 2024 fiscal year, one point three six billion. [T10] About three point seven million parcels a day.

> Chiffres : Temu + Shein, « more than 30 % » des colis de minimis (commission de la Chambre, 2023) ; e-commerce ≈ 50 % du fret aérien Chine → États-Unis (Xeneta).

A House committee estimated in 2023 that Temu and Shein together probably sent more than thirty percent of them. [T11] And e-commerce parcels filled about half of all air cargo capacity from China to the US, according to the freight analysts at Xeneta. [T27]

> Citation : « Temu conducts no audits ». Commission de la Chambre sur le PCC, juin 2023.

Those light checks worried lawmakers. In June 2023, the House Select Committee on China said that Temu "conducts no audits" of its suppliers for forced labor, and concluded there was "an extremely high risk" that forced labor had entered its supply chains. [T11] The committee called these findings preliminary.

> Schéma du système complet : usine → Temu (prix, logistique) → un colis par commande → de minimis → client. Financé par Pinduoduo ; poussé par la pub.

So here's the real answer. Not one trick, but a system. The factory sold directly. Temu set the price and handled shipping. Each order travelled alone, under the eight-hundred-dollar line, duty-free. And Pinduoduo's profits paid for the ads that filled the funnel.

Take away one piece, and the whole thing has to change. In 2025, Washington took away the parcel.

## 6. The door closes

> Chronologie : 2 mai 2025 · 29 août 2025 · 20 févr. 2026 · 24 juin 2026 · 1er juil. 2027.

On May second, 2025, the exemption ended for goods from China and Hong Kong. [T12] Postal parcels were first hit with a duty of one hundred and twenty percent, cut to fifty-four percent twelve days later. [T12]

On August twenty-ninth, 2025, it was suspended for every country in the world. [T16] And Congress had already voted to repeal it in law, from July 2027. [T16]

> Photo du Capitole. Texte : « Learning Resources v. Trump, Feb. 20, 2026 ».

The door might have reopened once. In February 2026, the Supreme Court ruled that the emergency law behind many tariffs could not be used to impose them. [T17] But the same day, the White House put the de minimis suspension on a different legal footing, and customs kept it "without modification". [T17]

> Sceau de la CBP. Chiffres : plus d'1 Md$ de droits, plus de 246 M d'envois.

In June 2026, US Customs wrote the suspension into its own regulations. [T18] It says it has collected more than a billion dollars of duties on more than two hundred and forty-six million shipments since the phase-out began. [T18]

> Texte : « Tariff rates: changed again and again. Duty-free entry: gone. »

The tariff rates on top have changed again and again, and may keep changing. [T19] But the key fact for Temu hasn't moved. A cheap parcel from China no longer gets into America for free.

So what did Temu do?

## 7. Plan B: warehouses and Europe

> Courbe : part de Temu dans les impressions Google Shopping : 20 % le 5 avril → 0 % une semaine plus tard.

First, it went quiet. In April 2025, its share of Google Shopping ads in the US fell from about twenty percent to zero in a single week. [T14] By May, Temu's daily users in the US had fallen by forty-eight percent compared with March, according to Sensor Tower. [T15]

> Citation Temu : « All sales in the U.S. are now handled by locally based sellers ». Mai 2025.

Second, it changed how it ships. In May 2025, Temu said that "all sales in the U.S. are now handled by locally based sellers, with orders fulfilled from within the country". [T13] It had started down that road in 2024, with factories shipping goods in bulk to American warehouses. [T7]

> Schéma : avant, un colis par commande depuis la Chine ; après, un conteneur vers un entrepôt américain, puis livraison locale.

But a container shipped to a US warehouse pays customs duty, like any other import. Someone has to rent the warehouse, hold the stock, and take the risk that it doesn't sell. That's exactly the cost the old model avoided. Prices can still be low. But the magic trick is gone.

> Carte : l'Europe s'allume. Chiffres : Europe ≈ 40 % des ventes, États-Unis ≈ 30 % (estimation d'analyste).

Third, it leaned on the rest of the world. According to analyst Ed Sander, Europe now makes up about forty percent of Temu's sales, and the United States about thirty. [T23]

> Photo du Berlaymont. Chiffres : 4,6 Md de colis de faible valeur en 2024, plus de 90 % de Chine ; 3 € par colis depuis le 1er juillet 2026.

But Europe is closing its own door. In 2024, four point six billion low-value parcels entered the European Union, more than ninety percent of them from China. [T20] Since July 2026, they pay a flat customs duty of three euros. [T20] On a five-euro item, that's a sixty percent surcharge.

> Chiffre : amende DSA de 200 M€, mai 2026. Réponse de Temu à l'écran.

Brussels is also watching what's inside them. In May 2026, the European Commission fined Temu two hundred million euros for failing to properly assess and limit the risk of illegal products on its platform. [T21] Temu disagrees, calls the fine disproportionate, and says it has since strengthened its systems. [T21]

> Citation : « lower fulfillment efficiency and higher costs ». Lei Chen, PDD.

PDD's co-chief executive, Lei Chen, has warned that cross-border orders in those markets will face "lower fulfillment efficiency and higher costs" in the short term. [T24]

## 8. So, can Temu survive?

> Barres : T2 2026 contre T2 2025. Chiffre d'affaires +8 %, bénéfice net −12 %.

PDD's latest results, for April to June 2026, show a business still growing, but more slowly, and paying for it. Revenue was up eight percent. The line that includes Temu grew thirteen percent. Profit fell twelve percent. [T6]

> Logos Amazon et Temu face à face ; photo d'entrepôt.

So here's our answer. Yes, Temu can survive without duty-free shipping. It has already lived without it in America for a year and a half. And its parent makes billions of dollars a quarter.

> Liste : stocks locaux · droits de douane payés · livraison plus rapide · prix moins spectaculaires.

But it can't survive as the same company. The Temu that shipped two-dollar parcels one by one from a factory in China, duty-free, is gone in America and fading in Europe. What's replacing it looks more like a regular marketplace: local warehouses, sellers who pay customs duty, faster delivery, and prices that are low, but no longer impossible.

> Logo Amazon. Texte : « Amazon Haul: items under $20, shipped from China ».

The strangest part? The old model worked so well that Amazon copied it, with a direct-from-China store of items under twenty dollars, just as the rules that made it possible were disappearing. [T25]

> Texte final : « The trick was never just the factory. It was the parcel. »

So the secret of Temu was never just cheap factories, or cash, or ads. It was a customs rule written in 1938, long before anyone could order socks from a phone. When the rule changed, the price of a parcel changed, and so did Temu.
