## 0. Hook

> Logo Red Lobster (usage informatif) sur une photo de restaurant. Chiffre héros « $20 », « all the shrimp you can eat ».

In the summer of 2023, Red Lobster made a promise that sounds like a dare. For twenty dollars, all the shrimp you can eat. Every day. Forever. [R12]

> Énoncé : « −$11 million, one quarter. »

It lost eleven million dollars on that deal in a single quarter. [R13] Less than a year later, America's biggest seafood restaurant chain filed for bankruptcy. [R18]

> Photo de restaurant fermé. Énoncé : « Killed by shrimp? »

So the story wrote itself. Red Lobster was killed by its own shrimp. Customers ate it out of business.

> Tuiles : « 30% fewer customers », « $190.5M in rent », « $76M lost in 2023 ».

But read the bankruptcy filing, and the shrimp shows up as the last blow, not the cause. The chain had lost thirty percent of its customers since 2019. [R16] It was paying around a hundred and ninety million dollars a year in rent. [R17] And it was losing money long before the all-you-can-eat deal went permanent.

The shrimp didn't sink Red Lobster. It was the last wave to hit a ship that was already taking on water.

To see where the water came in, we have to go back to Florida, in 1968.

> Ident The Cost Curve, 3 s.

## 1. The fish house

> Chronologie animée : 1968 Lakeland, 1970 General Mills, 1991 biscuits, 1995 Darden. Photo de Lakeland.

On January eighteenth, 1968, Bill Darden and Charley Woodsby opened a seafood restaurant in Lakeland, Florida. [R1] Darden had opened his first restaurant at nineteen. This one was different. It sold fresh seafood, at prices a family could afford, far from the coast.

> Photo d'un plat de fruits de mer.

It worked so well that by 1970, with just five restaurants, Red Lobster was bought by General Mills, the cereal giant. [R2] With General Mills money behind it, the chain spread across America through the eighties and nineties. [R2]

> Photo des biscuits. Énoncé : « Cheddar Bay Biscuits, 1991 ».

In 1991 came the warm garlic cheese biscuits that would later be called Cheddar Bay Biscuits. [R3] For a lot of Americans, that basket on the table was the reason to go.

> Compteur : « 700+ restaurants ».

In 1995, Red Lobster became the flagship of a new company named after its founder: Darden Restaurants. [R3] At its peak, there were more than seven hundred Red Lobsters. [R5]

For a generation, it was where you went for a birthday, a graduation, a promotion. Not fancy. But a treat.

That brand was so strong that even in 2016, when Beyoncé name-checked Red Lobster in a song, the chain said its sales jumped thirty-three percent. [R4]

But by then, something important had already changed. Red Lobster no longer owned the ground it stood on.

## 2. The sale

> Logo Darden. Chronologie : décembre 2013, pression des actionnaires ; 16 mai 2014, vente.

By the early twenty-tens, casual dining was getting squeezed, and Darden's shareholders wanted Red Lobster gone. [R6] In May 2014, Darden agreed to sell it to Golden Gate Capital, a private equity firm from San Francisco, for two point one billion dollars in cash. [R6]

> Schéma : 2,1 Md$ payés, 1,5 Md$ récupérés aussitôt.

Here's the detail that matters. As part of the same deal, the real estate under about five hundred Red Lobster restaurants was sold to a property company, American Realty Capital Properties, for one and a half billion dollars. [R7]

> Énoncé : « Sell the buildings. Rent them back. »

It's called a sale-leaseback. You sell the buildings, and you rent them back. The buyer got most of its money back right away. The restaurants got a landlord.

> Citation d'Eileen Appelbaum, Fortune, 2014.

One economist, Eileen Appelbaum, did the math in Fortune that summer. Golden Gate, she wrote, had "arranged to recoup seventy-one percent of the total investment before it even took control." [R8]

> Tuiles : « 25-year leases », « +2% a year ».

Most of those leases ran for twenty-five years. And the rent went up by two percent, every single year. [R7]

> Citation : « We have seen this movie before and it did not end well. »

Appelbaum had studied thirty restaurant buyouts from the years before. Ten had ended in bankruptcy. Her verdict on Red Lobster: "We have seen this movie before and it did not end well." [R8]

## 3. The rent

> Courbe : loyer +2 % par an, indice 100 en 2014, 120 en 2023 ; clientèle qui baisse.

Two percent a year doesn't sound like much. But it compounds. From 2014 to 2023, a rent rising two percent a year goes up by about twenty percent. [R7]

> Énoncé : « Rent goes up. Every year. »

And rent doesn't care how many people walk in. A restaurant can cut its menu, its staff, its opening hours. It can't cut its lease.

> Chiffre : « $190.5M » de loyers en 2023, dont 64 M$ pour des restaurants peu rentables.

By 2023, Red Lobster was paying about a hundred and ninety and a half million dollars in lease obligations. More than sixty-four million of that was for restaurants that weren't pulling their weight. [R17]

> Barres : clientèle 2019 = 100, 2023 = 70.

Meanwhile, the people stopped coming. By the time it filed for bankruptcy, Red Lobster had about thirty percent fewer customers than in 2019. [R16]

> Énoncé : « Fixed costs up. Customers down. »

That is the trap. Fixed costs going up every year, and customers going down. Sales did rise for a while after the pandemic, by about a quarter from 2021 to 2023, but mostly because menu prices went up. [R16] Fewer people, paying more, in restaurants that cost more to rent each year.

Then came an owner who also wanted to sell Red Lobster the shrimp.

## 4. The supplier

> Logo Thai Union. Photo de Bangkok ou d'élevage de crevettes.

In 2016, Thai Union, a seafood giant from Thailand, one of the biggest tuna canners in the world and a major shrimp supplier, bought a minority stake in Red Lobster for five hundred and seventy-five million dollars. [R9] In 2020, a group led by Thai Union bought the rest. [R9]

> Énoncé : « Your owner is also your supplier. »

Think about what that means. The company that owns the restaurants is also a company that sells seafood to the restaurants.

> Chronologie des PDG : 2021 Valade, avril 2022 départ, Kenny par intérim, Dawson, Tibus, Adamolekun.

Then the top job became a revolving door. The chief executive who arrived in 2021, Kelli Valade, left in April 2022. [R10] A Thai Union executive, Paul Kenny, ran the chain as interim boss. NPR reported that Red Lobster went a year with no permanent CEO. [R10]

> Chaîne : trois fournisseurs de crevettes panées, deux barrés, Thai Union seul.

According to the bankruptcy filing, Red Lobster then dropped two of its breaded shrimp suppliers under what the filing calls "the guise of a quality review". That left Thai Union with an exclusive deal, at a higher cost. [R11]

> Encadré : « Allegations from the 2024 filing. Thai Union called them meritless. »

Those are allegations. They come from the man who later ran the company through bankruptcy, and Thai Union called them meritless. [R18] But they set the stage for the decision everyone remembers.

## 5. The shrimp

> Photo de crevettes. Héros « $20 ». Puis tuiles « $20 → $22 → $25 ».

Ultimate Endless Shrimp was an old idea. For about twenty years, Red Lobster ran it each autumn, for a few weeks, to pull people in. [R12]

In 2023, it became permanent. Every day, for twenty dollars. [R12]

> Citation de Tibus (déclaration de faillite).

According to the filing, it was done "despite significant pushback from other members of the company's management team." [R12]

> Énoncé : « It brought people in. That was the problem. »

And it did bring people in. That was the problem. A deal like that is meant to attract guests who then order drinks, desserts and pricier plates. For a few weeks a year, it can pay. As an everyday price, the numbers told a different story.

> Chiffre : « −$11M » au troisième trimestre 2023.

The price went to twenty-two dollars, then twenty-five. [R12] It still lost eleven million dollars in a single quarter. [R13] Some restaurants ran out, the filing says, "often going days or weeks without certain types of shrimp." [R12]

> Citation de Ludovic Garnier, directeur financier de Thai Union, 2023.

Even Thai Union's own finance chief admitted it on an earnings call. "On this promotion, we don't earn a lot of money. At twenty-two dollars, we don't." [R14]

> Énoncé : « A symptom, not the disease. »

But eleven million dollars, on its own, does not sink a company with hundreds of restaurants. It made a bad situation worse, at the worst possible moment.

## 6. The bankruptcy

> Chronologie : janvier 2024 sortie de Thai Union ; mars 2024 Tibus PDG ; mai 2024 faillite.

In January 2024, Thai Union announced it was getting out. Red Lobster's "ongoing financial requirements," it said, "no longer align with our capital allocation priorities." [R15] It wrote down about five hundred and thirty million dollars, nearly everything it had paid. [R15]

> Citation de Jonathan Tibus.

A turnaround specialist, Jonathan Tibus, was brought in. In his sworn declaration, he wrote: "It was immediately clear that Red Lobster's performance was deteriorating and had been doing so for several years." [R16]

> Reçu : « RED LOBSTER, FISCAL 2023 » : net loss −$76M ; rent $190.5M ; customers since 2019 −30% ; shrimp promo −$11M (one quarter).

Here is the bill he found. A seventy-six million dollar net loss for 2023. [R16] About a hundred and ninety million dollars in rent. [R17] Thirty percent fewer customers than before the pandemic. [R16] And the shrimp, eleven million in one quarter. [R13]

> Photo de restaurant fermé. Compteur : « 93 closed ».

In the days before the filing, ninety-three restaurants were shut overnight. [R18] Then, in May 2024, Red Lobster filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, with more than a billion dollars of debt. [R18] About thirty-six thousand people still worked in its restaurants. [R18]

## 7. The comeback

> Logo Fortress. Photo du nouveau PDG non disponible : énoncé.

In September 2024, Red Lobster came out of bankruptcy, owned by its lenders, led by Fortress Investment Group. [R19] The new chief executive, Damola Adamolekun, came from P.F. Chang's. Born in 1989, he became the youngest boss in the chain's history. [R19]

> Citation : « we inherited a very damaged brand ».

He didn't sugarcoat it. "We inherited a very damaged brand," he told Fortune. [R20] He also said endless shrimp would never come back, "because I know how to do math." [R20]

> Chiffres : « +6.5% traffic, Oct. 2025 », « +10% sales, early 2026 ».

There were signs of life. Traffic rose six and a half percent in October 2025. Sales were up about ten percent a year later. [R20]

> Barres : restaurants, pic 700+, mai 2024 ≈ 580, août 2026 ≈ 540.

But the chain is smaller now. More than a hundred and sixty restaurants have closed since the bankruptcy, including Times Square, and about five hundred and forty remain. [R21] And in April 2026, endless shrimp came back after all, as a limited-time offer. [R21]

> Encadré : plainte de mai 2026 contre Thai Union.

The fight over what went wrong isn't over. In May 2026, a trust representing shareholders sued Thai Union in Florida, claiming it used its control to "squeeze out every drop of value that it could." [R22] Thai Union did not immediately respond to Fortune, and the claims have not been tested in court.

## 8. What really sank it

> Chronologie complète, cinq jalons : 1968, 2014, 2016, 2023, 2024.

So, let's put the timeline back together.

> Liste : 1. The buildings were sold. 2. The rent kept rising. 3. The customers left. 4. The owner sold the shrimp. 5. The shrimp was free.

In 2014, the buildings were sold, and the restaurants became tenants. The rent rose every year, while customers drifted away. An owner that also sold seafood took control. And only then, with the margin already gone, came twenty-dollar endless shrimp.

> Énoncé : « The shrimp was the last wave. »

The shrimp is the part we remember because it's funny. A chain eaten alive by its own customers. But the less funny part is what killed it: a business that had to pay more every year, for buildings it used to own, with fewer people coming through the door.

> Citation d'Adamolekun : « the greatest comeback in the history of the restaurant industry ».

Its new boss says this could be "the greatest comeback in the history of the restaurant industry." [R23] Maybe. But it starts with far fewer restaurants than the seven hundred it once had.
